1The Challenge

Northwood Logistics had outgrown their existing wide-aisle racking layout at their Crewe distribution centre. Three new e-commerce contracts, signed in late 2025, were due to onboard in Q1 2026 — collectively requiring around 6,500 additional pallet locations on top of their existing 11,500-pallet inventory.

Relocating to a larger building wasn't viable: the Crewe site sits inside a logistics park with established motorway access and a labour pool already trained on Northwood's WMS. The team needed to find roughly 60% more storage capacity inside the existing four walls — without taking the warehouse out of service. Their existing customers, including two major retail clients with daily SLA commitments, couldn't tolerate downtime.

The specific challenges were:

  • Hard capacity target: needed at least 18,000 pallet locations within the existing 9,500m² footprint to onboard the three new contracts.
  • Zero downtime requirement: the warehouse had to remain operational throughout — picking, putaway and outbound flows had to continue.
  • MHE fleet constraint: existing fleet of 8 reach trucks; switching to a fully automated system wasn't economically viable for an 18-month payback target.
  • Compliance burden: three of the incoming customers required full SEMA-certified racking with documented load notices, plus a fire protection upgrade signed off by a chartered fire engineer.
  • Aggressive timeline: all three new contracts required go-live by 1 March 2026, leaving an 8-week window from contract signature to operational handover.

"We'd quoted with three other suppliers. Rack City were the only ones who turned up to the survey with a draft layout already sketched out — and the only ones who committed to the 8-week handover in writing."

2The Solution

Rack City Solutions proposed a phased migration from wide-aisle to narrow-aisle (VNA) racking, allowing Northwood to add a guided VNA truck to their existing fleet rather than replacing it wholesale. The CAD design reorganised the warehouse into three zones, each migrated separately over a four-week install programme.

Key elements of the solution:

  • VNA racking system: 18,200 pallet locations across 24 runs of double-sided narrow-aisle racking, 8m high, designed for guide-rail VNA operation with 1.6m aisles.
  • Wire-guidance install: floor-cast wire guidance running the full length of each aisle, allowing the new VNA truck to operate at full speed without pedestrian-grade lane widths.
  • Phased zone migration: warehouse split into Zones A, B and C — each cleared, refitted and recommissioned in sequence, with stock rotated through the unaffected zones to keep operations live.
  • Integrated fire protection: in-rack sprinkler system designed and signed off by a chartered fire engineer, integrated with the existing dry riser system.
  • Load notices & SEMA sign-off: full documentation pack delivered at handover, including pallet load notices fixed to every aisle end frame and a SEMA Approved Installer certificate.
  • Operator training: half-day VNA familiarisation and rack-awareness training delivered on-site for Northwood's 18 reach truck operators.

3The Outcome

The project completed on Tuesday 17 February 2026 — two days ahead of the contracted handover date and 11 days before the first new customer's onboarding deadline. The three new e-commerce contracts went live on schedule, and Northwood reported no SLA breaches with either of the existing retail customers during the migration window.

Twelve months on, the system continues to perform as designed. Northwood have signed Rack City as their preferred racking partner for their planned Trafford Park expansion in late 2026.
